Fuchu, Tokyo

Kaikokan (TUAT West Tokyo International Innovation Co-creation Hub) — a co-creation hub on the Fuchu campus of Tokyo University of Agriculture and Technology

Take a bus from Fuchu Station on the Keio Line or Kokubunji Station on the JR Chuo Line to “Harumicho (Tokyo Noko Daigaku-mae)”. On the Fuchu campus of Tokyo University of Agriculture and Technology (TUAT) stands Kaikokan, the West Tokyo International Innovation Co-creation Hub. The ground-floor cafeteria and the 2nd-floor Three-University Co-creation Space are open to anyone, and the 2nd floor also has members-only coworking and private offices. Introduction

Where the region meets the world, woven by university knowledge

Kaikokan is a co-creation hub that Tokyo University of Agriculture and Technology set up on its Fuchu campus. Its main themes are agriculture, food and energy.

The ground floor has a cafeteria, a MUJI shop, an agriculture-and-food restaurant and an event space; the 2nd floor is the incubation space; and the 3rd floor has a lounge, an outdoor terrace and a solar-sharing demonstration area.

01 · Three-University Co-creation Space

A 2nd-floor learning space open to all

The 2nd-floor Three-University Co-creation Space is designed for active learning. It is open to everyone, including students of TUAT, Tokyo University of Foreign Studies and the University of Electro-Communications.

It is available from 8:00 to 21:00, and may close for events.

02 · Cafeteria

A ground-floor space where anyone can sit

The ground-floor cafeteria is a relaxed space open to all. You can eat food bought in the building, or bring your own.

MUJI and the agriculture-and-food restaurant are on the same floor.

03 · Shared office

Members-only coworking and private offices

The 2nd-floor shared office has 96 coworking seats, four private offices, meeting rooms and a test kitchen, available 8:00–23:00.

Individual membership is ¥22,000 a month and corporate membership ¥60,500 a month (four people). You apply after a tour and interview, and screening usually takes about a month. Meeting rooms are free for up to 30 hours a month.

What is solar sharing?

Installing solar panels above farmland to generate electricity while still growing crops, also called agrivoltaics.

At Kaikokan, solar sharing on the 3rd floor serves as a demonstration and research area for urban agriculture.

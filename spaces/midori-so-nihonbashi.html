Higashi-Nihonbashi, Tokyo

MIDORI.so Nihonbashi — a café and bar on the ground floor and a dormitory where you can stay while you work, in Higashi-Nihonbashi 3-chome

MIDORI.so Nihonbashi is in Higashi-Nihonbashi 3-chome, a one-minute walk from Bakuro-yokoyama and Higashi-Nihonbashi stations. With a café, bar and gallery on the ground floor, a lounge on the 2nd and semi-private dormitory rooms for working and sleeping on the 3rd and 4th, it is a place where working and staying live together. There is no sign-up fee or membership: you use it through day and night pop-in. Everything below is based on the official website.

Introduction

Work and rest, without hurrying

MIDORI.so Nihonbashi combines a lounge, dormitory-like rooms and a ground-floor café and bar. Working and staying share one building, which the website says supports a way of “working and resting gently, without hurrying”.

It is not membership-based: you use it through day pop-in (10:00–18:00) and night pop-in (18:00 to 9:30 the next morning). You can carry on working and simply stay the night.

01 · 1F café, bar and gallery

An entrance open to the street

The ground floor has a café, bar, gallery and reception. Guests, workers and neighbours all pass through here first.

Bakuro-yokoyama and Higashi-Nihonbashi stations are both a one-minute walk away.

02 · 3F–4F rooms for working and sleeping

A semi-private dormitory

The 3rd and 4th floors are semi-private rooms for working and sleeping. Night pop-in costs ¥8,800 per night.

Rooms for working and sleeping on the same floor are rare even among coworking spaces.

What is a dormitory?

A shared, hostel-style way of staying, where several people sleep in the same room or on the same floor. At MIDORI.so Nihonbashi, the 3rd and 4th floors are semi-private dormitories for working and sleeping.

Adding a place to sleep to a place to work changes things for people who come from far away — or who are up against a deadline.

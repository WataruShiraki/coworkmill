Takebashi, Tokyo

Chiyoda Platform Square — a shared office in a renovated Chiyoda City public building, connecting generations and regions

Chiyoda Platform Square is in Kanda-Nishikicho, a two-minute walk from Takebashi Station. Born in 2004 as a pioneer of coworking and shared-office culture in Japan, it was created by renovating a Chiyoda City public building. It has free-address “Open Nest” seating, lockable “Closed Nest” units, meeting rooms that can host talks for 200 people, and a rooftop garden. Everything below is based on the official website.

Introduction

A shared office where generations and regions connect and grow

The official website presents it as “a shared office where many generations and regions connect and grow” — a base where people of different ages and places create new business and culture together.

It was born in 2004 as a pioneer of Japan's coworking and shared-office culture, by renovating a Chiyoda City public building.

01 · Lobby and wooden deck

A bright lounge beside a café

Through the entrance, a bright, open lounge welcomes you. A café next door makes it handy for meeting up or quick discussions.

Beside the main building is a wooden deck surrounded by greenery, used for meetings and community events.

02 · Open Nest

Pick a seat to suit the day

Open Nest is a free-address workspace where you choose a seat to suit your mood or task that day.

With just a laptop, you can start work straight away.

03 · Closed Nest

Lockable private units

Closed Nest units are lockable private workplaces, ranging from about 3.4 m² to 53 m².

Landlines are available, and security is at a level that supports gaining Japan's PrivacyMark certification.

04 · Meeting rooms and rooftop garden

Up to talks for 200

Meeting rooms on B1F, 4F and 5F handle everything from internal meetings to talks for around 200 people.

A rooftop garden for tenants only even has a vegetable patch.

What is a non-profit-type company?

A company that takes the form of a joint-stock company but does not aim to pay out profits as dividends, instead using its results for the local community and society.

Chiyoda Platform Square is run by Platform Service Co., Ltd., a non-profit-type company.

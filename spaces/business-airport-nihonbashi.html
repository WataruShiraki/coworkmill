Nihonbashi, Tokyo

Business-Airport Nihonbashi — a workplace connecting people and places, with interiors that evoke the starting point of the Five Routes

Business-Airport Nihonbashi, run by Tokyu Land, is on the ground floor of Nihonbashi Front in Nihonbashi 3-chome. Its interior evokes Nihonbashi as the starting point of the Five Routes that once linked Edo to the whole country, and the website says it was created to connect people with people and people with places. Everything below is based on the official website.

Introduction

From Nihonbashi, the great merchant district, to a new stage

The slogan is “From Nihonbashi, the finest merchant town of its age, to a new stage”. With an interior that recalls Nihonbashi as the starting point of the Five Routes, the website says it has created a new workplace that links people with people and people with places.

A shared workplace, three meeting rooms, an online meeting room, an event space, a solo work area and serviced offices: places for different ways of working line the ground floor.

01 · Shared workplace

A shared space recalling the start of the Five Routes

The shared workplace is open 7:00–22:00 on weekdays (reception 9:00–18:00) and 10:00–18:00 on Sundays and holidays.

Address membership is ¥82,500 a month and Master ¥38,500.

02 · Solo work area and serviced offices

Seats for one, rooms for teams

The solo work area is for focused individual work.

Serviced-office ROOMs start at ¥176,000 a month and DESKs are ¥104,500. They can be entered 24 hours a day.

03 · Meeting rooms and event space

Three meeting rooms and a place for events

There are three meeting rooms (A, B and C), an online meeting room and an event space.

On the ground floor of a merchant district, it serves as a place where people meet.

What were the Five Routes?

The five highways laid out in the Edo period with Nihonbashi as their starting point: the Tokaido, Nakasendo, Koshu Kaido, Nikko Kaido and Oshu Kaido.

Business-Airport Nihonbashi echoes this starting point in its interior. The brand's idea of a place people depart from overlaps with Nihonbashi's history.

Chitose-Funabashi, Tokyo

Tatami Works — coworking in tatami rooms, three minutes from Chitose-Funabashi Station

Tatami Works is a coworking space in Funabashi 1-chome, Setagaya, a three-minute walk from Chitose-Funabashi Station on the Odakyu Line. As the name suggests, it is a workplace that incorporates tatami. The ground floor is for conversation, and the 2nd floor for focused work, with both floor seating and chairs. Everything below is based on the official website.

Introduction

A place to turn “what if” into reality

Tatami Works is a coworking space whose motto is “a place where you can give shape to your ‘what if’” — somewhere to turn the ideas you picture into real things.

Its facilities page notes that tatami rooms help regulate humidity, and that igusa, the rush used in tatami, is said to have a relaxing effect. It suits remote work, reading and exam study, and is used by a wide range of people including freelancers, homemakers and students.

01 · Ground floor

A relaxed floor where you can talk

The ground floor is a space for working in comfort, with a work area, community space, bookshelves and lockers.

Eating is allowed (nothing strong-smelling or noisy), and Zoom and phone calls are fine.

02 · 2nd floor

A focus floor with floor seats or chairs

The 2nd floor is for focused work, with both floor seating and chairs.

You can eat here, but phone calls and Zoom are not allowed.

03 · Equipment and prices

Free equipment and monthly plans by time of day

Monitors, chargers, mice, keyboards, laptop stands, desk lamps, a whiteboard, a printer and blankets are free to use (limited numbers). Wi-Fi runs on a line of up to 10 Gbps.

Drop-in is ¥500 an hour or ¥2,500 a day. Monthly plans are ¥22,000 for full access, ¥16,500 for weekday daytime, ¥12,100 for nights and ¥8,800 for mornings, and there is an address service usable for company registration (¥5,000 a month).

What is igusa?

A rush plant used to weave the surface of tatami mats, known for its distinctive scent.

Tatami Works highlights working in a tatami room, and mentions that igusa is said to have a relaxing effect.

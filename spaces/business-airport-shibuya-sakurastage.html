Business-Airport Shibuya Sakura Stage — a Shibuya workplace where people, culture and the world cross paths, on the 7th floor of SHIBUYA Tower

Business-Airport Shibuya Sakura Stage, run by Tokyu Land, is on the 7th floor of SHIBUYA Tower at Shibuya Sakura Stage, close to Shibuya Station. Under the slogan “the future gathers in cutting-edge Shibuya, overflowing with creative sensibility”, it aims to be a space where body and mind feel at ease in the middle of an exciting city. Everything below is based on the official website.

Introduction

In Shibuya, where people, culture, value and the world intersect

The website writes that “the future gathers in cutting-edge Shibuya, overflowing with creative sensibility”. In Shibuya, where people, culture, value and the world intersect, it aims to offer a rare space where body and mind can feel at ease.

The shared area is called the “SHIBUYA Well Center Workers' Lounge” and is open on weekends and holidays as well as weekdays. Private rooms, fixed desks, meeting rooms and an event space are all on the same floor.

01 · Workers' Lounge

A shared space open on weekends too

The Workers' Lounge is open 7:00–22:00 on weekdays (reception 9:00–18:00) and 10:00–18:00 on weekends and holidays.

Address membership is ¥99,000 a month, Master ¥38,500, Private ¥14,300, and Flex ¥550 per 30 minutes.

02 · Serviced offices

ROOM and DESK

Private ROOMs start at ¥198,000 a month and fixed DESKs at ¥143,000.

Serviced offices can be entered 24 hours a day.

03 · Meeting rooms and event space

A room for six, and a place for events

Meeting room A for six costs ¥2,750 an hour, and the one-person online meeting room ¥550 per 30 minutes.

The event space costs ¥33,000 an hour for members and ¥49,500 an hour for non-members.

What is the Workers' Lounge?

The name of the shared workspace at Business-Airport Shibuya Sakura Stage, officially the “SHIBUYA Well Center Workers' Lounge”.

Putting the word “well” in the name shows the intention: a place to rest body and mind in stimulating Shibuya.

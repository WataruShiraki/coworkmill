What is the difference between a coworking space and a shared office?

Coworking means sharing open seats; a shared office means renting a lockable room. In practice, many places offer both — co-ba ebisu has coworking on the ground floor and private offices on the 2nd.

co-ba ebisu →

Shared seats downstairs, lockable rooms upstairs

co-ba ebisu has coworking on the ground floor and private offices on the 2nd. Downstairs, a free-address membership usable 24 hours a day costs ¥30,000 a month, and a fixed BOOTH seat ¥65,000.

Upstairs, rooms of about 31–72 m² are rented on two-year fixed-term leases. In one building, there is a way in for renting a seat and a way in for renting a room.

LIFORK Otemachi →

13 types of room, from 4 to 30 people

LIFORK Otemachi's shared offices come in 13 room types, from rooms for 4 to rooms for 20–30. They are available 24/7, with utilities included in the rent.

Each company rents its own room; the joining fee is one month's rent and the deposit 1.1 months'. It is a clear example of a shared office, contracted by the room rather than by the seat.

co-lab Shibuya Cast →

From booths for 1–2 to rooms for 22

co-lab Shibuya Cast is a shared office for creators, with 16 booths for 1–2 people, 7 studios for 3–5, 8 ateliers for 4–12 and 4 rooms for 12–22.

Flex membership with no fixed seat starts at ¥15,000 a month, and a single desk at ¥42,000. With room sizes so finely graded, both individuals and small teams can find a fit.

ARCH, Toranomon Hills →

CO-WORK and SMALL OFFICE on the same floor

ARCH's CO-WORK is “a varied workspace where you can choose where to work to suit the situation”, with power and network at every seat.

SMALL OFFICE is a secure second office. The same 3,800 m² floor holds both open seats and closed rooms.

What is a shared office?

An office where a building is divided into rooms rented by several companies or individuals, with reception, lounges and meeting rooms shared.

Where the line with coworking falls differs by place. A space centred on shared seats is usually called coworking; one centred on lockable rooms, a shared office.
